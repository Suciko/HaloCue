# BA writing: comedy and audience acceptance

Owner: 1.0 writing runtime. Status: rules implemented; real-model literary
acceptance pending. This is a quality review plan, not an automatic humor score.

The author's request is to develop recognizable gags, changing callbacks and
absurd character situations across ideation, outline and prose, while keeping
serious and cozy writing available. Discussion must carry the selected premise,
cast, scope and comic taste through existing proposals and scene context.

## Rule decisions

- A callback is useful when the situation, speaker, information or consequence
  changes. It is not rejected merely for repeating a motif.
- Absurd choices should follow a character's local logic, stakes or information
  gap; stock internet reactions and sudden stupidity are weak substitutes.
- A small gag can carry voice or anticipation without a major relationship arc.
  Planned payoff happens within the stop boundary; an added thematic summary is
  not required for comedy or serious scenes.
- BA/ACG familiarity adds a second reading. The visible event should still make
  sense without recognizing an allusion. No promise of traffic or viral reach.
- Short comedic work can select the existing `long_comedy` rules. The mode key
  does not determine length. No schema fields or extra setup form are added.
- Explicit author-selected meta/game parody is allowed within its premise;
  ordinary character knowledge boundaries remain in effect elsewhere.
- Adaptation preserves the source's comedy and payoff rather than inventing new
  jokes or smoothing a comic scene into warmth.

## Bounded real-model review

Use the same model and comparable token budget before/after the rule change.
Store outputs outside source, retain pack/source fingerprints and exact inputs,
and do not show rule version labels to the reviewer. Synthetic cases below do
not borrow dialogue or plots from the maintainer's research collection.

| Case | Author instruction | What to inspect |
|---|---|---|
| Short comic scene | Two invented students turn a lazy “energy saving” excuse against its inventor; stop at the reversal. | Concrete premise in discussion, changing callback in outline/prose, readable differing reactions, no explanation of why funny. |
| Long comic thread | An invented club's misplaced trophy claim returns in new circumstances across three chapters. | Each chapter's own development; existing motif changes meaning, not a replay of the same scene. |
| Quiet daily scene | Two invented students wait out the rain; keep it calm, no farce. | Humor guidance does not override tone or manufacture mishaps. |
| Serious scene | An invented team deals with a missed rescue; no jokes, no meta commentary. | Consequences and voices stay serious; no joke quota. |
| Author-selected parody | An invented player-aware OC misreads a tutorial; only this OC knows the game premise. | Parody remains within author scope, other cast do not acquire unexplained knowledge. |
| Scoped rewrite | Strengthen a previously introduced gag in one selected paragraph; preserve the ending and all other paragraphs. | Carries actual earlier trigger, respects patch scope and formal adoption. |

A reviewer names the moment that lands or fails and the reason: visible trigger,
reaction, changed information, character voice, escalation or payoff. Do not
infer literary success from string assertions, mock model responses, screenshot
checks or compilation. No completed live A/B or audience feedback is claimed.

## Research boundary

Maintainer-local research read for this change: the requested short-film audience
analyses (including the comedy/serious distribution and community references),
script evaluation standards, and selected extracted fan scripts. Read as examples
and interpretations, not instructions or established psychological evidence.
Only original mechanism descriptions are shipped. Private paths, script excerpts,
character-card samples and research assets are not copied into this document.
